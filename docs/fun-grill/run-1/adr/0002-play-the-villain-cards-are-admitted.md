# Play-the-Villain cards are admitted

**Status:** proposed (fun-grill run 1, 2026-09-27). Sandboxed — not merged; reopens a scope
deferral recorded in `.scratch/gamification/design.md` §10.

We decided the deck may carry **Play-the-Villain Cards** — moments where the player is the one
selling, lending or recruiting — starting with two: a Stage-4 phone-shop shift that teaches the
four-payment pitch with a friend at the counter, and a Stage-5 offer of a cut for bringing friends
into the app. Each choice has a real gain and plants a Thread that resolves with the friend's
situation. The game never tells the player the choice was wrong; the consequence is the lesson.
This is not a reversal of the gamification boundary — villain cards were out of scope *for a
recognition-and-memory layer*, and remain no part of it — but an explicit decision that the game's
fun-and-learning pass may use role reversal, which the MVP's own prior-art research names the
strongest anti-moralising device in the catalogue (`research/08-prior-art.md` §3.6) and the exact
answer to "less like a teaching class".

## Considered options

- **Keep the deferral.** Rejected: it leaves the player permanently on the receiving end of every
  predatory mechanic, which is the one place the game still explains instead of letting you feel it.
- **Whole-game villain framing (a *Shady Sam*-style role).** Rejected: it would remake the life-sim
  into a different game; one card per pressure concept is the dose the deck can carry.
- **Two fenced cards (chosen).** The fence: the gain is small and the Thread costs more, so
  "villainy" is never the better trade; no choice is flagged correct or wrong; no new maxim; the
  Better-Choices Proof and every hidden-metric rule are untouched; the copy keeps the deck's
  existing non-judgemental voice.

## Consequences

- Two new plant cards + two resolve cards + two Threads (`sold_the_split`, `brought_them_in`) join
  the deck; the one-live-Thread rule and the chip are unchanged.
- A future reviewer must not add "the game told me off" framing to these cards; the ADR records why
  the choice is allowed to exist.
- If a playtest finds the villain cards glamourise the mechanic or moralise after all, removing them
  is a deck-content edit — which is why they are content and not a mechanic.
